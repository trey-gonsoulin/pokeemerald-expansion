# Balance: exp formula, story bosses and level curve

The fork's balance changes and the tools that model and apply them. The commands for `tools/area_archetypes` are in `CLAUDE.md`.

- Exp formula configs (#154), fork code in `src/exp_formula.c` (`include/exp_formula.h`), called from `ApplyExperienceMultipliers` in `src/battle_script_commands.c`, on in the fork's `include/config/battle.h` and listed in `BATTLE_CONFIG_DEFINITIONS` (`include/constants/config_changes.h`) for `WITH_CONFIG`. The owner picked them in the curve explorer (#151):
  - `B_SCALED_EXP_STEEP`: the level-scaled exp uses `gExperienceScalingFactorsSteep` (level^3.5) instead of level^2.5.
  - `B_EXP_OVERLEVEL_PENALTY`: above the fainted Pokémon's level, the exp follows a logistic curve in the percent of its level the getter is over it (#189, replacing #154's halving).
    - Its shape: full exp at the foe's level, falling slowly at first, fastest at `B_EXP_OVERLEVEL_CUTOFF` (15) percent over `B_EXP_OVERLEVEL_WIDTH` (4) percent (0: straight to the floor past the cutoff), then levelling out at `B_EXP_OVERLEVEL_FLOOR` (10) percent of the exp. The result is at least 1, and no exp stays none.
    - `ApplyOverlevelPenalty` runs after every other multiplier, so it covers Exp. Share bench and catch exp too.
    - It's integer math: `sExpLogistic`, 4096 / (1 + e^t) in 16ths of t, interpolated in 256ths, then one u64 division. `tools/rebalance`'s `Overlevel.apply` computes the same, and both test suites pin the same cases.
    - The cutoff and width are at most 255 and the floor at most 100: the test override stores them in 8 bits (static assert).
  - Covered by the "Exp formula:" tests in `test/battle/exp.c`. Tests that pin other parts of the formula turn the penalty off with `WITH_CONFIG`, since a level-20 mon gets a tenth of the exp from a level-10 foe: three upstream tests at the top of that file, the Lucky Egg test in `test/battle/move_effect/embargo.c`, `WITH_EXP_AWARD_CONFIGS` and `test/battle/hold_effect/exp_share.c`'s `WITH_EXP_SHARE_CONFIGS`. Add the same to a new test that measures exp across a level gap.
  - `tools/rebalance` reads both configs (`game_scaling`, `Overlevel.from_configs`; off in its vanilla regime), so `report.md` and the explorer's defaults follow them.
- Story boss levels raised for the Gen 6 Exp. Share (#127), levels only, in `src/data/trainers.party` and Steven's in `src/data/battle_partners.party`. These are fork data changes:
  - The owner's rule: balance with the Exp. Share on and the 1.5x trainer exp off, mainly by raising opponent levels, never with a level cap.
  - Each boss's ace sits 1 above the modelled Exp. Share bench on a full clear. The Elite Four step one level per fight, and Wallace sits 3 above Drake.
  - `tools/rebalance/` (standard library, imports the area_archetypes parsers) models the curve. `python3 tools/rebalance/rebalance.py` regenerates `output/report.md` and `output/curve_data.json`, which CI checks for drift, so rerun it after any `trainers.party` or wild-table change.
  - `python3 tools/rebalance/rebalance.py explore` serves the curve explorer (#151): the same model in the browser through Pyodide (CDN, needs internet), with sliders for the rotation, the exp-formula options (including an over-level cutoff or falloff) and #150's targets (in levels or percent), and a chart of the boss, party, route-trainer and wild levels. `rebalance.py curve '<settings JSON>'` prints the same run. The explorer's `.js` files need `git add -f` (the repo ignores `*.js`). `output/findings.md` is hand-written: the analysis, the targets and the owner's decisions. A rival level change has to go on all six teams of that fight (May or Brendan with each starter), and boss raises also move the Mossdeep partner battle and the post-game rematches.
  - `tools/rebalance/power.py` (a prototype, report only): each story boss Pokémon's effective level, the level of a typical Pokémon (base 80, IV 15, no type) that fares as well one-on-one on turns to KO, from its base stats, IVs, moves and certain abilities. The report's "Combat power estimate" puts it against the Exp. Share bench, and `findings.md` section 15 reads it. Nothing (`apply`, the explorer) uses it yet.
  - `tools/rebalance/matchup.py` (a prototype, report only): each story boss against a stand-in player team per starter (the starter line plus the best new-type catches by then, with coverage moves from default movesets and earlier stages' TMs, by `tools/rebalance/matchup_settings.json`), on `power.score` with the type chart. The report's "Matchup-relative strength" gives each fight's hardest boss Pokémon and its best answer, coverage and the lead's answer, and `findings.md` section 16 reads it. The rival teams follow the starter suffix (`_TREECKO` is the team met after choosing Treecko). `rebalance.py` imports it, so it is in the explorer's `FILES` (`explorer/model.js`), as `power.py` is; a test fails on a local module missing there.

## Wild tables

`tools/area_archetypes/README.md` documents the archetype rules and the restock options. The committed wild species are vanilla: `src/data/wild_encounters.json` is the restock with `tools/area_archetypes/restock_settings.json` (`{"ratio": 0}`, so only the level offsets apply), which `rebalance.py apply` and the pinning unittest pass as `--config` too. #28 had committed the default 0.5 restock (Gen 4-9 species such as Frillish on Route 110), and the owner turned it back for now; to restock again, edit that file and rerun `restock --write --config tools/area_archetypes/restock_settings.json`. `build` and `restock` read the committed vanilla snapshot, never the live `src/data/wild_encounters.json`, so `restock --write` reruns don't compound. Restock also applies the wild level offsets in `tools/area_archetypes/level_offsets.json` (#150), which `rebalance.py apply` writes.
